## Key Patterns Observed

### Pattern 1: Google Prefers "Tell Me About a Time" Format
The overwhelming majority of G&L questions are situation-based, asking for specific past
experiences. Generic hypothetical answers score poorly.

### Pattern 2: Google Also Asks Reflective Questions
Unlike Amazon (pure STAR), Google sometimes asks open-ended reflective questions like
"What do you think about X?" The recommended strategy (per interviewing.io) is to convert
these into situational answers grounded in real experience.

### Pattern 3: The Five Core Signals
Based on InterviewBit's definition and multiple sources, Google evaluates Googleyness on:
- Doing the right thing even when it is hard
- Comfort with ambiguity and imperfect information
- Genuine collaboration over individual heroics
- Intellectual humility (admitting what you do not know)
- Continuous learning and curiosity

### Pattern 4: Leadership = Influence Without Authority
For IC (individual contributor) roles like L4 SWE, leadership is NOT about managing people.
It is about:
- Influencing technical decisions through data and empathy
- Mentoring and growing others
- Taking ownership of problems nobody assigned you
- Driving initiatives from idea to completion

### Pattern 5: Behavioral Round Is De-Emphasized but Still Scored
Per interviewing.io's ex-Google interviewer guide, the behavioral round is the "least
important" of the four interview loops and is sometimes skipped entirely. However, when
it does happen, it carries equal weight in the hiring committee packet. A bad G&L score
can sink an otherwise strong candidate.

### Pattern 6: Most Frequently Asked Topics (by frequency across sources)
1. **Conflict/Disagreement** -- appeared in every source

3. **Leadership without authority** -- appeared in every source

## What Google Is NOT Looking For (Anti-Patterns from Sources)

- Blaming others for failures
- Taking all credit on team projects
- Claiming you have never failed
- Generic answers without specific examples
- Using "we" for everything (interviewer cannot assess YOUR contribution)
- Badmouthing previous employers
- Not showing growth or learning from experiences
- Rehearsed-sounding robotic answers
